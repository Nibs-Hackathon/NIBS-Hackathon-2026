# Incident Response Playbook

## Universal first response

Recognize the abnormal condition, protect people, establish communication, confirm the affected equipment, and follow the site emergency or alarm-response procedure. Use independent indications where practical. Preserve evidence and timestamps. Do not send personnel into a potentially hazardous area without the required gas testing, protective equipment, permit, and authorization.

## High pressure

Compare upstream and downstream pressure, flow, control-valve demand, relief status, temperature, and equipment mode. Possible contributors include downstream restriction, blocked outlet, control failure, thermal expansion, abnormal vapor generation, or erroneous indication. Stabilize only through approved operating actions. Do not isolate a relief path or defeat high-pressure protection.

## High temperature

Check correlated temperature instruments, process flow, cooling or quench availability, heater duty, reaction conditions, pressure, composition, and recent operating changes. Possible contributors include loss of cooling, low flow, exchanger fouling, excessive heat input, reaction acceleration, or sensor failure. Escalate rapidly when temperature rise is sustained or accelerating.

## Gas detection

Treat a credible gas alarm as a potential loss of containment. Establish the affected area, wind direction, ignition controls, detector agreement, ventilation state, and personnel accountability. Follow the site gas-release procedure and emergency classification. A cleared detector does not by itself close the incident.

## High vibration

Review vibration direction and spectrum when available, speed, load, bearing temperature, lubrication, suction conditions, coupling state, and process transients. Rapidly increasing vibration, rubbing indications, or bearing-temperature rise may require prompt shutdown under the equipment procedure.

## Low or restricted flow

Confirm valve line-up, pump or compressor state, suction conditions, filter or strainer differential pressure, downstream pressure, inventory, and instrument validity. Avoid forcing additional differential pressure across a suspected blockage without an approved plan.

## Incident closure

Operational normalization, investigation completion, and administrative closure are separate states. Close the physical incident only after readings remain stable within the approved envelope and field condition is confirmed. The execution report should remain linked to the incident, evidence, asset, facility, agent findings, recommendations, and operator decisions.
