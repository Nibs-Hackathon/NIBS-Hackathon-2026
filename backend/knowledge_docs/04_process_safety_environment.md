# Process Safety and Environmental Controls

## Barrier management

Track prevention, detection, control, mitigation, and emergency-response barriers. Examples include mechanical integrity, alarms, trips, relief systems, gas detection, fire protection, drainage, secondary containment, operating procedures, competency, permits, and emergency response. An impaired barrier changes the risk picture even when production remains normal.

## Permit to work and isolation

Work on process equipment requires the site permit and isolation standard. Confirm equipment identity, energy sources, process hazards, depressuring and draining, electrical isolation, stored energy, gas testing, lockout or tagout, boundary integrity, and handback requirements. Software status alone does not establish physical isolation.

## Management of change

Changes to equipment, chemistry, control logic, alarm settings, operating limits, procedures, materials, organization, or temporary configurations require screening under the site management-of-change process. Temporary changes need an owner, expiry, risk review, documentation, and restoration plan.

## Relief and flare systems

Monitor header pressure, knockout drum level, seal condition, pilot status, smokeless capacity, radiation constraints, and known simultaneous relief scenarios. Do not interpret visible flare reduction as proof that the initiating process condition is resolved.

## Emissions and wastewater

Correlate emissions with unit conditions, analyzer quality, fuel or feed changes, combustion performance, wastewater loading, tank movements, and maintenance activity. Regulatory limits and reporting obligations are site- and jurisdiction-specific; consult the controlled compliance register.

## Emergency information

Emergency response must use the site emergency plan, alarm classification, muster and accountability process, mutual-aid arrangements, and public authority interfaces. This corpus provides context but is not a substitute for site emergency instructions.
