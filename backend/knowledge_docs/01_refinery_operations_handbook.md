# Refinery Operations Handbook

## Purpose and authority

This handbook is a decision-support reference for trained refinery personnel. Site operating procedures, permits, equipment manuals, alarm-response procedures, and the control-room operator remain authoritative. Never use this reference to bypass an interlock, defeat a protective system, or improvise an operating limit.

## Process areas

A typical refinery includes crude receipt and storage, crude and vacuum distillation, treating and desulfurization, conversion units, hydrogen and sulfur recovery, product blending, tank farms, utilities, flare and relief systems, wastewater treatment, marine or pipeline transfer, and electrical distribution. Unit interactions matter: a utility disturbance can affect many process units even when their local instruments initially appear normal.

## Shift handover

Handover should identify unit mode, constrained equipment, inhibited or impaired safeguards, open permits, overrides managed under site procedure, temporary repairs, active alarms, abnormal trends, laboratory exceptions, product movements, weather exposure, and work that changes process containment. The incoming operator should confirm critical information against the control system and current permits.

## Safe operating envelope

An operating envelope is defined by approved limits for pressure, temperature, level, flow, composition, vibration, emissions, and equipment condition. Treat a persistent approach toward a limit as meaningful even before an alarm occurs. Validate the instrument, compare independent indicators, check upstream and downstream behavior, and determine whether the trend is process-wide or isolated to one sensor.

## Alarm response principles

Prioritize personnel safety, containment, and stabilization. Confirm the alarm source, review correlated measurements, maintain communication with field personnel, and follow the site alarm-response procedure. Do not reset a trip or restart equipment until the initiating condition is understood and the responsible authority approves the action.

## Production and energy

Production rate, yield, energy intensity, steam balance, fuel gas balance, hydrogen balance, flare loading, cooling demand, and wastewater load should be evaluated together. A local optimization that increases fouling, corrosion, relief loading, or off-spec inventory is not an operational improvement.

## Data quality

Telemetry is evidence, not certainty. Mark stale, substituted, flat-lined, out-of-range, or contradictory measurements. A value without timestamp, unit, tag identity, calibration state, and operating context must not be presented as verified live data.
