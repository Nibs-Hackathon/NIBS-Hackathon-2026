# Rotating and Fixed Equipment Reliability

## Pumps

Review suction conditions, discharge pressure, flow, vibration, bearing temperature, seal condition, motor current, lubrication, and minimum-flow protection together. Common contributors to poor pump performance include suction restriction, vapor formation, loss of prime, recirculation, misalignment, imbalance, bearing degradation, seal damage, and operation away from the preferred range. Never treat reduced vibration alone as proof that a pump is healthy.

## Compressors

Monitor suction and discharge pressure and temperature, vibration, lube-oil condition, seal system, anti-surge margin, driver load, cooling, and gas composition. A compressor upset can progress quickly. Follow the approved anti-surge and trip-response procedures; do not manually force operation across a protective limit.

## Heat exchangers

Evaluate inlet and outlet temperatures, pressure drop, duty, approach temperature, flow distribution, leakage indicators, fouling history, corrosion circuits, and cleaning history. Falling duty with rising pressure drop often supports fouling, but instrumentation error, bypass leakage, flow maldistribution, or changing feed properties must also be considered.

## Vessels and tanks

Verify level using independent indication when inventory is safety-critical. Monitor pressure or vacuum protection, vent condition, temperature stratification, water bottoms, roof or seal condition, corrosion monitoring, transfer line-up, and overfill safeguards. Tank movement requires positive identification of source, destination, available capacity, and transfer authorization.

## Piping and valves

Assess corrosion circuit, wall-thickness history, supports, vibration, dead legs, injection points, small-bore connections, insulation condition, leak history, valve position indication, actuator health, and passing isolation. A closed indication does not independently prove positive isolation.

## Boilers and turbines

For boilers, consider feedwater quality, drum level, flame stability, draft, fuel pressure, steam conditions, emissions, and trip status. For turbines, consider speed, vibration, bearing temperature, lube oil, steam conditions, overspeed protection, and driven-equipment load. Restarts require completion of the approved readiness checklist.

## Maintenance evidence

Use condition history, inspection findings, failure modes, work-order quality, parts availability, production consequence, and safety consequence to prioritize work. A maintenance recommendation should name the evidence, uncertainty, required isolation, responsible discipline, and acceptance criteria.
